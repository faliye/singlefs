### What was done

1. Timing wrapper device: wraps the memory disk (`SparseBlockDevice`) or the real disk (an `O_DIRECT` image file on ext4, `DirectInputOutputBlockDevice`), reads `Instant` and this thread's `clock_gettime(CLOCK_THREAD_CPUTIME_ID)` once before and once after every kind of `BlockDevice` call (the CPU reading enclosing the wall-clock reading), and accumulates wall clock, CPU, disk wait (wall clock minus CPU), count and bytes per call kind; reads are split by "before / after the first write in this timing window", and reads before the first write are split into three columns: those landing in R on R's disk, minus re-reads on the relocation-source slots, go to scan reads; those on this batch's relocation-source slots (same-numbered slots on both disks), minus what remains after the first read of each slot on R's disk, go to the pre-release disk-read check (`release_check_read_bytes`, registration section twelve 12.5); the rest go elsewhere; writes are split into "landing on this batch's destinations / elsewhere". The positive-control injection (sleep 2 ms in every `write_at`, or spin 2 ms by the thread CPU clock) also lives here.
2. Pool building: with harness default parameters, mkfs two 4 GiB memory disks, the first file, writable mount; create P + 64 empty inodes, the first P each sequentially writing one data unit; in segments written entirely by this run, overwrite 3 out of every 4 (f = 0.25) or 1 (f = 0.75) in slot order, and in mixed segments overwrite all units written by this run; fill the highest segment with the extra empty inodes; then "raise F to the acceptance limit and create one inode" until all the overwritten old placements are released. **The order of the overwrites (change Jia, apparatus `overwrite_order_of`, line 2131)**: f = 0.75 still goes entirely in ascending order of old slot number; f = 0.25 is changed into three groups in turn: ① units not in a segment "whose 32 units were all written by this run" (all of the mixed segments, and those named by the recipe's accumulation), in ascending old slot number within the group; ② the units to overwrite in the full segments, except the full segment with the highest slot numbers, in ascending old slot number; ③ the 24 units to overwrite in the full segment with the highest slot numbers, in ascending old slot number. Which units are overwritten, how many times, and what is written stay the same; only the order changes. Mechanism (registration section seven "inferred prediction"): every overwrite is one publish and writes one root, the root ring has 24 roots, and the old placement is recorded as released by the publish of that overwrite, so from the 25th overwrite on every copy lands in a hole punched 24 overwrites earlier; therefore the holes punched by the last 24 overwrites (the 24 in the full segment with the highest slot numbers) are still empty when building ends, and that segment is left with 16 live slots, all written by this run.
3. Geometry check: on a copy, writable mount and replay the segment-choosing loop of the session's issuing step (`choose_region` alternating with "scan first") to see which segment greedy will choose; if that segment is all data units written by this run with live slots = 64f, use greedy; if not but it is all data units written by this run, fold them into the recipe and rebuild (at most three times); if it holds other units, take the registered fallback (the test-only forced segment choice naming the f segment with the lowest slot number). After pool building, print the composition segment by segment (`name=segment_composition`, N11), the formed-segment counts from two paths side by side (`counts_agree` of `name=segment_composition_summary`), and where the overwrite copies landed (`name=overwrite_landing`, N12); these lines are printed only for the first pool building of each point, not for the rebuilds of rounds 2–5.
4. Measuring one intent: writable mount (`mount_writable_with_compaction_switches`; the product switch only swaps the batch size for the test-only `ForcedForTests(K)`), set the session's "publishes already elapsed" to 1000 before every call (the publish budget drops to 1), and call `run_compaction` once at a time until the last batch deletes the intent; read `Instant` and the thread CPU clock before and after every call (the wall-clock reading enclosing the CPU reading, the reverse of the device-call layer), and after the return, outside the timing window, judge from the version produced whether it was the issuing call, a batch, or the last batch. Before and after every intent, outside the timing window, also do one calibration (reading 1, N15) and the thread and machine readings (reading 3–N9, `name=intent_machine_readings`): an intent whose calibration ratio is > 1.10 is voided; after voiding, calibrate every 10 seconds and measure again only after two consecutive results ≤ 1.10, waiting at most 300 seconds at a time and at most 600 seconds in total within one phase (revisions R2-1, R2-2); at the start of every phase take the calibration baseline (reading 2, 21 passes) and the doubled-work self-check (R2-3), and take the baseline again at the end of the phase (R2-4). One `name=machine_state` line (N10) each at the start of the phase, at the start of every sample point, and at the end of the phase.
5. Replay in phase two: before the call, take this version's allocation records, mapping entries (a separate copy of the session's private way of getting them), and pinned and quarantined slots, and the bytes of the units the scan read; three stages (Jia: whole-unit CRC-32C of every unit read (the other sub-item is the whole-unit CRC-32C of the copies of the pre-release disk-read check, whose bytes are read before the call, one copy from the same-numbered slot of each disk); Yi: the whole `scan_region`; Bing: every `choose_region` of the issuing step), each replay first running once untimed as warm-up, then once timed, taking the median of 5 replays; saturated throughput at 1, 16 and N threads, 2 seconds each. The stage Bing judgement (change six): each round takes the `choose_share_per_batch` already computed on the issuing line of that round's valid intent, judges it against the original threshold (10%), and the five rounds are collected into one `name=choose_judgement` line (the `name=stage_judgement` lines are still printed, and their stage Bing cell is still `not_run`).
6. Phase three (reused, not run in this pass): on each card, using one real data unit (32768 bytes) written during pool building, with n from 1 to 16384 ranges, `GpuCard::crc32c_of_ranges` is run once and discarded, then timed 5 times and the median taken, checking that every range's CRC matches the CPU's and that no run is faster than the PCIe link peak; a and b are fitted by least squares, φ and n* are computed with phase one's B_批, and the ratio in the form of D24 (Offloading Heavy Background Work to GPU) settled item 3 (Q9) is computed with phase two's 16-thread saturated throughput.
7. Single-point check (the `geometry-check` subcommand, change two): only builds the pool and checks the geometry, printing the `name=geometry_attempt`, `name=geometry`, `name=segment_composition`, `name=segment_composition_summary`, `name=overwrite_landing` and `name=pool` lines and the last line, without measuring; `--overwrite-order slot-order` builds the way the first rerun did (everything in ascending old slot number, without counting where copies land).

### Measurement basis

- CPU time is the difference between two readings of this thread's `clock_gettime(CLOCK_THREAD_CPUTIME_ID)` (registration section one, revised in section twelve 12.2; the old schedstat reading advances only about once per 1 ms for a running thread, measured numbers in 12.2). `wall-clock`, `stage-split` and `foreground-fsync` first spin 1 ms on this thread while reading this clock continuously, and if it advances fewer than 100 times they stop and exit 3 (artifact line `name=clock_resolution_check`). In the device-call layer the CPU reading encloses the wall-clock reading, and disk wait = wall clock − CPU, which can be negative in a pure-CPU device call (the time spent reading the clocks); in the layer of one `run_compaction` call the wall-clock reading encloses the CPU reading. Nested this way, when the two clocks run at the same speed, "wall clock ≥ CPU + disk wait" cannot be broken by the order in which the clocks are read.
- A batch's wall clock is the `Instant` enclosing that one `run_compaction` call (including the discard at the end of the call); discard, barrier and zero-writing are listed separately by call kind.
- Real-disk arm: before every measurement, copy the 4 MiB blocks changed by the previous measurement from the starting copy back into the working copy, then check that the block digest of the whole image (the SHA-256 of every 4 MiB block, concatenated and hashed with SHA-256 again) matches the starting copy; the memory arm rebuilds from the same recipe every round and checks that the block digests of both disks match the first build. At mount time, the hint for the journal record offset copies the sectors written before the unit area (answered the same way as for the memory disk), rather than reading the whole ring slot by slot on the `O_DIRECT` image; there is no such copying inside the timing window.
- Device: the memory arm is the harness's sparse memory disk; the real-disk arm is two image files on ext4 on an NVMe drive of the second host (model CT2000T500SSD8, logical block 512, `research/results/e185-r2-inputs-wall-clock-2026-10-23.txt` lines 22–26), the barrier is `fdatasync`, and the conclusion only speaks of "`O_DIRECT` image files on ext4 on that disk of the second host" (rerun registration section one "real disk"); the disk of this host named in the original registration was not used.
- Batch: a call that relocated units (the same measurement basis as the session's `observations.batches_published`); the issuing call is listed separately and is not counted as a batch.
- Q4's `rises` only requires the ratio of the first batch's wall clock to be > 1 (5-round median, and > 1 in every round); in this pass the medians of the 12 pairs are 1.015102–1.074115, a small rise, and the criterion does not look at how much it rose.
- "A batch's CPU" for Q7 is the median of the batch CPU of that intent (the apparatus's reading since the original registration, pinned in registration section six "how stage Bing gets a reading"); the stage Bing value is `choose_share_per_batch` on the `name=stage` line of the issuing call, one per round from that round's valid intent, so each `name=choose_judgement` cell has only 5 values.
- First-row positive control (row 1 of the positive control table in registration section five; the CPU arm's disk-wait sentence follows section twelve 12.9): sample point f = 0.75, P = 1024, K = 8, sleeping 2 ms in every `write_at` (disk arm) or spinning 2 ms by the thread CPU clock (CPU arm). Every increment is the median of the control rounds − the median of the baseline rounds. Disk arm: the disk-wait increment of the whole intent falls within [1.0, 1.5] times the number of write calls × 2 ms, and the CPU change is within ±10% (unchanged). The CPU arm reports two sentences separately: the CPU increment falls within ±10% of the number of write calls × 2 ms (unchanged); the absolute value of the disk-wait increment of the write calls (the call kind where the injection happens) ≤ number of write calls × 2 ms × 10% (the new judging rule of 12.9, the denominator being the injected amount). The original criterion "the change in disk wait of the whole intent relative to the baseline is within ±10%" is still computed and reported (`wait_change`, `wait_clause_under_the_original_criterion_holds`) but not used for judging. In every round at the control point, each device arm measures the baseline, the disk-arm control and the CPU-arm control once each back to back, in an order rotated by round number (rounds 1 and 4 baseline → disk arm → CPU arm, rounds 2 and 5 disk arm → CPU arm → baseline, round 3 CPU arm → baseline → disk arm), 5 rounds; the median of the increments paired by round number is reported separately and not judged.
- Voiding and stopping (registration section eleven): the eight voiding rules of the original registration, item ③ of 12.10, and the first rerun's "counting feature pulled in" are kept; revision R2-1 adds a third trigger (one of the calibration ratios before and after this intent is > 1.10, `invalid_reason=calibration_ratio_above_the_registered_edge`), R2-2 waits for normal after voiding before retrying, R2-3 stops if the calibration's own positive control fails (exit 4), and R2-4 only marks baseline drift; stop clause 7 (the apparatus changed: the first line of the inputs list does not match the registered hash, and revision R2-8 also checks it inside the apparatus) through stop clause 10 (calibration anchor mismatch), and revisions R2-5 and R2-7 tighten to stopping (exit 3) when the cumulative number of passes with an unreadable baseline or an anchor mismatch exceeds 3; revision R2-6 records `invalid_reason=calibration_unreadable` for an intent whose calibration cannot be read. None of these triggered in this pass (both phases' verdict lines `stop_clause_findings=0`, and the 140 + 60 intents are `invalid_reason=none`).
