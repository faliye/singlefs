### Criteria and Failure Clauses Hardcoded Before Run

1. Three arms report recovery count and overhead cell by cell across identical set of faults.
2. **Positive control run arm by arm**: when fault length is 0, recovery count across all three arms must equal total units; if any arm fails, entire round is invalidated.
3. **Negative control**: entire disk destroyed, all three arms give recovery count 0.
4. **Proof 5 pinned to absolute values**: when chain arm destroys exactly one unit, recovery count is **exactly** 1 less; if "all lost after break point" appears, shape was chosen incorrectly.
5. **Proof 2**: count "detected but cannot recover" separately, making net increase visible.
6. **Discriminative power**: three arms must give different recovery counts on at least one fault point.

**Failure clauses**: if recovery count of chain arm equals no-redundancy arm across **all** fault points, record faithfully that
"chain arm buys detection not recovery", forbidding altering criteria or modifying shape to make it look better.

### A Structural Conclusion Drawn from Actual Run: Ditto Placement Policy Resonates with Periodic Faults

The placement policy of `separated by at least 1/8 disk` causes the three replicas to be **congruent and die together** when the fault period evenly divides 1/8 disk ——
E118 (Three Arms of Single-Disk Self-Recovery) has `DITTO_SPREAD = 32768 = 2^15`, and **any power-of-2 period divides it evenly**:
at k = 8 cell ditto is cell-by-cell identical to no-redundancy arm (both 4586), switching to k = 9 pulls them apart immediately (5242 vs 4659).
⚠️ **Periodic faults are a pattern manufactured by E118 (Three Arms of Single-Disk Self-Recovery) itself; real bad sectors are not periodic**;
this cell demonstrates that "taking power of 2 for placement offset incurs resonance risk", **not** that "ditto is useless under scattered faults".

### A True Blind Spot Forced Out by Mutation Testing

`M4_scatter_period_inverted` (changing `s % k == 0` to `!= 0`, turning destroyed portion from 1/k to (k−1)/k) **did not turn a single test red** ——
before adding `scatter_recovery_counts_are_pinned_to_absolute_values`, those scattered assertions **were all mutual comparisons between arms**, without a single one pinning absolute values; when all three arms erred together mutual comparisons remained equal.
Triaged per `.claude/rules/mutation-sampling.md`, this is a **true blind spot** rather than sampling point insensitivity: after inversion absolute values change on any sampling point.
After adding `scatter_recovery_counts_are_pinned_to_absolute_values`, all 12 mutations were caught.

### What It Cannot Answer

Pure counting model: **no real I/O, no crash-point replay**, does not model spatial correlation of bad sectors (bad sectors on real disks are not uniformly random).
Does not answer "what metadata ratio is in this project" —— swept as parameter. Does not answer ditto write amplification and how allocator guarantees "separated by 1/8 disk".
Deterministic model, **running N rounds demonstrates absence of hidden state, not statistical stability**.
