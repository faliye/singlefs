## E26 Lookup Cost and Accuracy of Three Accounting Models — Run (2026-08-29)

[中文](26-三种记账模型的查找代价与精度.md) · **English** · [日本語](26-三种记账模型的查找代价与精度.ja.md)

**Conducted for [decisions.md](../decisions.md) D6 (Snapshot Implementation Model).** The user questioned the btrfs numbers
cited by D6 (Snapshot Implementation Model) (commit time +76%, transaction wait +1347%, write throughput −25%) and requested retesting.

⚠️ **E26 (Lookup Cost and Accuracy of Three Accounting Models) does not retest btrfs, for mechanistic reasons**: the root cause of btrfs qgroup slowness is its own
backref resolution (tracing all referrers backwards from an extent), something that does not exist in our design.
Retesting btrfs merely yields btrfs's numbers. [prior-art.md](../prior-art.md) already labeled that set of numbers as
"Workload and hardware not fully disclosed; cannot be treated as performance expectation for this project, only as qualitative evidence"—that labeling is correct.
**E26 (Lookup Cost and Accuracy of Three Accounting Models) measures the algorithmic cost itself**: how many reverse index lookups deleting a snapshot requires, and whether reclamation accuracy is correct.

### Measured Results (64 Snapshots × 8 Extents per Snapshot, Deleting the Middle One, Byte-identical across Three Rounds)

| Topology | Sharing Rate | `deadlist` Lookups | `refcount` Lookups | `owner_ref` Erroneous Frees |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unidirectional Linear | 0 / 30 / 70% | **0 / 0 / 0** | 8 / 10 / 28 | 0 / 2 / 5 |
| Multi-writable Head | 0 / 30 / 70% | **0 / 3 / 13** | 8 / 12 / 23 | 0 / 0 / **8** |

### Scaling Is the Conclusion, Absolute Values Mislead

⚠️ **The table under "Scaling Is the Conclusion, Absolute Values Mislead" / "Measured Results" is a toy world of 64 snapshots × 8 extents, where "13 lookups" sounds very cheap—that is an illusion.**
Sweeping total extent count (fixed 70% sharing rate):

| Total Extents | Linear Topology | Multi-writable Head | Exact Reference Count |
|---|---|---|---|
| 256 | **0** | 1 | 13 |
| 512 | **0** | 13 | 23 |
| 1024 | **0** | 17 | 52 |
| 2048 | **0** | 33 | 65 |
| 4096 | **0** | 58 | 169 |
| 8192 | **0** | **213** | 379 |

**log-log slope: multi-writable head 1.32, exact reference count 0.95, linear topology constant 0.**

⇒ **The true difference is not "13 vs 23", but "constant 0" vs "O(N)".**
Under multi-writable heads, the D5 (Snapshot / Space Accounting Mechanism) model falls into the same complexity class as reference counting, with merely a slightly smaller constant factor—
**the very thing it eliminated is gone**, which was its entire raison d'être.

⚠️ **Limits of Extrapolation**: The model only ran up to 8192 extents (10⁻⁵ magnitude of realistic scale);
the 1.32 slope carries noise, and **how much I/O a single reverse index lookup costs is beyond the reach of E26 (Lookup Cost and Accuracy of Three Accounting Models)**.
Linearly extrapolating to 10⁸ extents yields "tens of millions of lookups per snapshot deleted"—**that is extrapolation, not measurement**.

**Three Conclusions**:

1. **The O(1) advantage of D5 (Snapshot / Space Accounting Mechanism) under unidirectional linear topology is independent of sharing rate**—at 70% sharing it remains **zero lookups**.
   This is what it genuinely buys, not being "slightly faster".
2. **Under multi-writable heads, that advantage degrades with sharing rate**: at 70% sharing deadlist pays 13 lookups, refcount pays 23.
   Still cheap, but **no longer O(1)**. This and E18 (Does D5 Break Down Under Multi-branch / Multi-writable Heads)
   "What D5 (Snapshot / Space Accounting Mechanism) breaks under branching is the O(1) property, not correctness" are two ways of measuring the same phenomenon.
3. **Paying lookups ensures neither loss nor leakage**: deadlist and refcount in all 6 cells have 0 `wrong_free` and 0 `leaked`.
   ⇒ **D5 (Snapshot / Space Accounting Mechanism) does not leak under multi-writable heads, provided one is willing to pay those lookups**;
   the 29.40% leakage measured by E26 (Lookup Cost and Accuracy of Three Accounting Models) is the variant that "refuses to pay" (which is bit-for-bit identical to "not recording death at all").

### ⚠️ Measurement Basis: `owner_ref` Arm Is Not btrfs squota

This arm models "**attributed to original creator, and freed when creator is deleted**".
**btrfs squota is not used this way**—it governs only quota attribution, **playing no part in block liveness determination**; computing it wrong merely makes quota inaccurate.
Importing this mechanism into free decisions leads to erroneous frees (E26 (Lookup Cost and Accuracy of Three Accounting Models) measured multi-writable head + 70% sharing erroneously freed all 8),
and **erroneous freeing lies in the direction of data loss**.
⇒ The conclusion of this arm is "**attributing to original creator cannot be directly used for free determination**",
**not** "squota loses data". The two must not cite each other interchangeably.

### Measurement Basis and Known Limitations

- Pure counting model, no device, no file system. Counts **reverse index lookups**, not time—
  how much a lookup is worth in a real implementation is beyond the reach of E26 (Lookup Cost and Accuracy of Three Accounting Models).
- Ground truth independently computed (enumerating all live snapshots), sharing zero code with the three arms.
- Deletes only a single snapshot, **without modeling cumulative effects of sequential deletions**.
- Code `scripts/experiments/e26_accounting.rs` (`cargo run --release --bin e26_accounting`),
  raw output `e26-accounting-2026-08-29.out`. Three rounds byte-identical.

### Pitfalls in the 08-29 Round: Modeling on Impression Once More

The first version of the `deadlist` arm **modeled death rules without reading the definition sentence of D5 (Snapshot / Space Accounting Mechanism)**,
yielding `wrong_free > 0`—directly contradicting the already-proven "data loss direction is structurally zero" of E26 (Lookup Cost and Accuracy of Three Accounting Models).
`rules/verify-before-claiming.md` records the exact same failure
("checked status column, did not read definition"), repeated here once again.
Fix: paste the definition sentence of D5 (Snapshot / Space Accounting Mechanism) **verbatim into code comments**, then model according to it.

The second pitfall was caught by mutation testing: the first version of "comparable set" only included ancestors, so descendants in linear history
were charged as side branches—whereas under linear history everything is comparable, which should have been zero lookups.

**Engineering Quality**: 10 unit tests, **all 8 mutations caught**, three rounds byte-identical.
