### Conclusions

1. **Dedicated units remain the most expensive form**: the most intuitive implementation (one tombstone per unit) pushes batch deletion to 65 536 000 000 bytes (61.04 GiB, artifact `phys_mib=62500.0` namely 62 500 MiB) per million files across both record granularities; this figure is independent of record width, byte-for-byte identical between Run 1 and Run 2 (Q8).
2. **Range records (one per object) + packed carrier remain cheapest in bytes**: rm -rf 1M packs into 1227 units (80 412 672 bytes, 76.7 MiB), vs 1716 units in Run 1 (112 459 776 bytes, 107.2 MiB); truncate 4096 extents with one range record per object consumes 1 unit, 4096× cheaper than per_extent × dedicated and 6× cheaper than per_extent × packed.
3. ⚠️ **Cost of packing lies in lifetime, not bytes**: reclamation granularity = unit ⇒ entire unit pinned until last record in unit eligible for release — identical shape to pin_stripe in E80 (Partial Release of Stripes). Quantifying this awaits reclamation rules (D18 (Information Carried in a Block) settled item 8 note acknowledged "when eligible for reclamation" unanswered), E83 (Granularity of Tombstones) does not fabricate numbers; v1 does not reclaim tombstones (D18 (Information Carried in a Block) settled item 24 clause 4), pinning does not occur in v1.
4. Dropping record width from 56 to 40 raises packing capacity from 582 to 815 (both taking header 136); header changing from 91 to 136 reduces capacity by 1 (816 → 815). Packing ceases to write less only at record width 16317 bytes or wider; 40 bytes sits far from it.
