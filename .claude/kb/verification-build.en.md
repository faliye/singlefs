## 1. What the Three Mechanisms Are

| Mechanism | Stage in gate | Input | Output | Authoritative definition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Transaction layer** (object under test) | No standalone stage; model differential, crash-point replay, and QEMU workloads all take it as a prerequisite | mkfs parameters, sequence of operations, a block device | Image, and a stream of write requests (for crash-point replay) | Project `CLAUDE.md`: "The first runnable goal is correctly committing a single transaction"; structural constraints in closed enum `CommitStep` in settled item 10 of D13 (Verification Route) and waistline in D17 (Implementation Layering & Third-Party Pipeline) |
| **checker** | Common adjudicator for both model differential and crash-point replay stages, corresponding to O2 (independent parser + checker) | **Single** image | True / False / Not applicable / Partially judged for each invariant (`InvariantVerdict::PartiallyJudged`: not counted as true, merged into not applicable in the crash statistics; the measurement basis is in the opening note of [invariants/README.md](../../crates/singlefs-checker/invariants/README.md)) | Settled items 6 and 7 of D13 (Verification Route): unary predicate, judging a single image only, strictly forbidden from invoking any deserialization or traversal code of the crate under test; [invariants/README.md](../../crates/singlefs-checker/invariants/README.md) is its specification |
| **Crash-point replay** | "Crash-point replay" stage | Stream of write requests (including barriers) + pre-crash image | Results of "Recovery → checker → Record checker" on each crash state | `.claude/singlefs-ai-sop/rules/test-discipline.md`: "Crash consistency can only be verified by crash-point replay"; layering and pruning in settled item 9 of D13 (Verification Route) "how crash-point replay is layered" |

Model differential (O1 (in-memory model differential with HashMap ideal file system) and RefFS in settled item 3 of D13 (Verification Route)) is not among the three named by the user, but constitutes a fourth prerequisite chain: it requires the checker to judge images and the transaction layer as the subject under test.
O3 (independent specification executor) (a specification executor re-derived in another language solely from the text of decisions and invariants) is also not among the three; it is the furthest from code among the three oracles in settled item 6 of D13 (Verification Route), placed at the very last step in landing sequence.
The record checker (its semantic inputs are the pre-crash image, the record stream, the persisted set (which writes reached the disk in this crash state) and the two post-crash images (the one in the crash state and the one after recovery, identical for a read-only recovery), intentionally left unnumbered in settled item 7 of D13 (Verification Route); its code entry point is `check_records_against` in `crates/singlefs-checker-tier/src/crash.rs`) is likewise not among the three, yet acts as the second adjudicator in crash-point replay—without it, the 7 classes of states where "root is present but records are absent" (E77 (Persistence Ordering of Publishes)) could only be discovered indirectly via single-image criteria in the checker.

**Two directions**: Traversal direction walks down from root slots; scan direction linearly scans the entire disk by steps defined in settled item 9 of D18 (Information Carried in a Block), claiming blocks via the 105-byte unit header (probes in E86, claim rules in E88, fixtures running on the 91-byte header at the time). Both judge the same invariant once each, sharing only format parsing (compromise in "Conflict 1" of settled item 12 of D13). I-1.2 (block header write order published) is the criterion of the scan direction independent of traversal, and the sole criterion separating torn transaction garbage from live units.

**Decision width must be probed independently, never taken from harness or implementation**: Criteria for I-7.5 (root slots aligned to the judged width) and I-8.2 (Record Header Does Not Cross Atomic Unit) use `physical_block_size` probed at mount time, which does not exist in the image. When the checker reads bare devices directly (criterion of O2), it must probe on its own and compare against the value recorded in system configuration at mkfs time (checker-side form of "per-device recalculation and comparison at mount" in settled item 2 of D22; slot width does not enter system configuration field table—user settlement on 2026-09-10: "slot width = probed physical_block_size at mount", checker compares probed vs actual slot width without reading fields). When files act as devices and cannot be probed, the checker must report geometry as "from argument" rather than as probed (pointed out by deduction leg).

**Independence is a hard constraint, not a styling preference**: The checker is a standalone crate, independent of implementation crates; linking implementation accounting or checksum code is forbidden (item 5 in D17 debt table, C12). The only permissible sharing between it and implementation is a single constants module generated from the KB (settled item 5 of D13, Question 2 in "Pending Decisions"); `singlefs-checker` depends solely on `singlefs-format`.

**It must prove itself capable of failing**: Each implemented invariant is paired with at least one image "corrupted just enough to trigger it", formatted like mutation tables in `scripts/mutation/tables/`, rerun by the gate (exactly what C13 requires: ran, all green, but no longer doing work looks identical to never having run). Dual root images in C88 and half-slot offset images in C47 were the earliest two entries in this corpus.

### Crash-Point Replay v1: User-Space Recording, Full Enumeration, Three Adjudicators

**Recording happens in user-space, not in the kernel.** The implementation is a Rust library with all I/O passing through a single block device abstraction; wrapping a recorder around that abstraction captures the full write request stream with barrier and FUA tags. `dm-log-writes` is reserved for C6—asking whether real block layer FLUSH / FUA semantics match our assumptions, which only real devices can answer; host and VM kernels both carry this module (see "Environment Inspection").

**Enumeration follows the E77 model** (torn subsets are not enumerated, settled in settled item 4 of D13; ⚠️ torn states are merged into "not persisted" for counting, but torn byte patterns must physically be generated as images fed to checker scan direction—a state where header landed but payload did not is precisely what I-1.2 catches; "merging into one bin for counting" must not be interpreted as "do not generate such images"): Barriers divide the write stream into segments; crash state = preceding segments fully persisted + arbitrary subset of current segment persisted + subsequent segments wholly unpersisted. Two barriers plus root slot FUA in settled item 7 of D16 (Publish Semantics) slice a publish into three segments; recording per device in new-pool file creation with arbitrary full-write subsets per segment yields 16,777,268 states (`FULL_STATES` in `crash_enumeration_new_pool_file_creation_stream.rs`; taking two states per write is 16,777,243; `--full` in gate checker-tier-crash-point-replay runs all states in release mode). **Crash-point replay does not sample; it enumerates fully**. E77 provides three reusable code segments: enumerator `crash_states`, recovery model `recover`, and audit `audit` with **unshared recovery code**—the discipline of separating auditor from auditee was learned from a bug caught by mutation testing there.

**Recording is per device, not per pool**: In v1 with two disks, every write has w ≥ 2 (settled item 6 of D2), journal ring mirrored across two disks (settled item 8 of D22); each unit and record represents two physical writes that can persist independently, and barriers represent two device FLUSH operations. Hence state counts 72 / 79 / 513 / 1024 from E77 cannot be used as harness assertions; the harness must calculate its own closed-form formula based on its write stream (pointed out by deduction leg).

**Each crash state executes three checks**: Implementation's own recovery → checker (unary judgment) → record checker (binary judgment, comparing pre-crash image, record stream, persisted set, and the two post-crash images). All three are independent; missing one leaves a class of errors unmonitored. All three are wired up: recovery + oracle, pool-level checker (`walk::check_pool_image` in `crates/singlefs-checker`, the count being the length of `IMPLEMENTED_INVARIANTS`, count measurement basis in section "Status" of `crates/singlefs-checker/invariants/README.md`; initial 29 invariants: 23 in v1 plus I-3.8 (Instance Table Rows Unique and Below Mount Root), I-7.4 (Blocks in Recent K Generations Not Reused), I-4.8 (Root Checksums Self-Consistent Within K Generations), I-3.9 (Release Generation Falls Within the Interval Where Reference Stopped), I-9.14 (Tree Table Entry Birth txg Invariant Across Roots), I-5.4 (Slots Covered by Allocation Records Mutually Disjoint)), record checker (`crash::check_records` in `crates/singlefs-checker-tier`), crash-point replay full run in gate checker-tier-crash-point-replay.

⚠️ **Crash-point replay workload cannot consist solely of "new-pool file creation"** (on 2026-09-14 only it was available): After mkfs, writing one file and publishing once releases zero blocks on data side, giving C22 and C80 test cases nothing to catch (pointed out by local and forward legs). Fixed workload must include overwrite, release, rollback and reuse—overwriting the same file generates one release, one delayed reuse, and one accounting change; C42 further requires "crash → recovery → short continuation → crash again". Thus fixed script comprises three publishes sandwiching one recovery: mkfs, publish A (write file), publish B (overwrite), recover at each crash point of B, publish C (short), recover at each crash point of C.
