## D25 Target Workload Priority —— Settled

[中文](25-目标负载优先级.md) · **English** · [日本語](25-目标负载优先级.ja.md)

D25 (Target Workload Priority) governs the cell of **which workload to prioritize**: two measurable values of the target workload (how many leaves one fsync carries, and how many disjoint spines those leaves land on), weights of each workload, along with modeling disciplines and measurement basis constraints when applying these two values to deduce other decisions. Out of scope: how index structures are chosen per access pattern is in D8 (Core Index Structure); answers to random write / fragmentation are in D10 (True Answer to Random Writes / Fragmentation) and D26 (Background Compaction and Placement Reclamation); whether to retain message buffers and what value to take for ε is in D11 (Message Buffers in Index Nodes); defer window and tail placement are in D16 (Publish Semantics) and D23 (Journal Role & Format).

#### Settled Item 1: Target workload adopts coarse granularity, seq prioritized

**Settlement**: The two target workload values—**one fsync carries 8 leaves, landing on 1 shared spine**, matching the `seq` tier in E16 (Journal Role: WAL vs Intent Log).

| Workload | Leaves/fsync | Spines | Write Amplification per fsync | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **seq Sequential Large Files** | **8** | 1 (shared) | **1.75** | **60** |
| metaheavy Metadata Intensive | 2 | ~1.5 | 4.97 | 10 |
| rand Full Range Random | 1 | 1 | 7.00 | 10 |
| multistream Multi-stream Concurrent | 1 | batch size | 7.00 | 10 |
| **smallfile Small File Intensive** | **Unmeasured** | **Unmeasured** | **Unmeasured** | **10** |

Leaving three columns blank for smallfile reflects reality: the measurement basis for these three columns comes from the leaf/fsync model of E16 (Journal Role: WAL vs Intent Log), whereas E114 (General Ledger for Small Data Packing Containers) measures object size and how many small objects land per publish; **the two are on different axes**, and numbers from E114 (General Ledger for Small Data Packing Containers) must not be inserted into these three cells. This row settles only one thing: **the weight of small file intensive workload in this project is 10 (seq 60 prioritized)**—giving D27 (small-data packing container) a denominator, while implying that a permanent format branch serves only a secondary workload, requiring independent justification under `.claude/rules/fs-design.md` branch criteria.

**Scope**: The four numbers in the table follow block-count measurement basis (Settled Item 5); adopting coarse granularity itself does not unblock format freeze for D8 (Core Index Structure) nodes—what blocked freeze was Candidate B of D10 (True Answer to Random Writes / Fragmentation), which no longer blocks after D26 (Background Compaction and Placement Reclamation) Settled Item 4 settled it as not entering format (storing no group identity, modifying no internal index node layout). This cell is product judgment, not experimental conclusion: experiments provide "what numbers each workload tier has", while "who to optimize for" is decided by humans (Settled Item 6).

**Basis**:

- E16 (Journal Role: WAL vs Intent Log): Four workloads verified cell by cell in the same model with zero fitting; leaf count, spine count, and write amplification for `seq` tier are all measured; numbers across the four rows all originate from that page.
- User settlement (2026-08-29 adopted coarse granularity; 2026-09-07 added smallfile row), verbatim words in changelog.
- User settlement 2026-09-24: Five workload weights (D25 (Target Workload Priority) Settled Item 6 "who to optimize for can only be settled by humans", verbatim words in changelog).

**Owed**: C174 (Three columns of smallfile row unmeasured).

#### Settled Item 2: Primitive variables are leaf count and spine count, not stream count

**Settlement**: Block count per fsync is given by the following formula—

> **Block count = leaves touched in one fsync + Σ distinct nodes touched at each level + 1 root slot + 1 record**

⇒ **Primitive variables are two**: ① how many leaves one fsync carries; ② how many disjoint spines those leaves land on. **Stream count is not a primitive variable; it acts through ②.** "Single stream / multi stream" must not be used as an axis of workload classification.

**Scope**: The formula is written per geometry of E16 (Journal Role: WAL vs Intent Log) (HEIGHT 4, one operation touches one leaf); changing geometry requires recomputing terms; it counts blocks, not barriers or bytes (Settled Item 5). Distinct nodes touched in hot regions for metaheavy calculated by probability, not back-solved.

**Basis**:

- E16 (Journal Role: WAL vs Intent Log): Four workloads predicted and matched cell by cell under `arm=intent`, `ckpt_interval=1000`, zero fitting; batch 10 cells also matched—formula contains no tunable parameters, excluding anything beyond the two primitive variables.

**Owed**: None.

#### Settled Item 3: Batch = 1 is degenerate cell, stream count visible only when batch > 1

**Settlement**:

| Batch | Non-user Blocks in One fsync | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | seq / rand / multistream **all identically equal 6.00** | One operation touches only 1 spine ⇒ 6 = HEIGHT 4 + root slot 1 + record 1. **Stream count completely invisible** |
| **10** | seq **6.50**, rand 32.65, multistream **33.00** | 10 operations of seq share 1 spine; 10 operations of multistream touch 10 disjoint spines ⇒ **5× gap** |

⇒ **Looking solely at batch = 1 concludes "stream count is unimportant", which is caused by modeling degeneration**, not a property. Any inference judging stream count importance from batch = 1 cells must first replenish batch > 1 cells.

**Scope**: This item addresses modeling sampling points, not altering the tier selected in Settled Item 1 (target values of seq: 8 leaves / 1 spine naturally reside on batch > 1 side). 6.00 and 6.50 / 32.65 / 33.00 are numbers under E16 (Journal Role: WAL vs Intent Log) geometry; changing geometry requires recomputation.

**Basis**:

- E16 (Journal Role: WAL vs Intent Log): Batch 1 and batch 10 run simultaneously; non-user blocks across three workloads identical at batch 1, widening 5× at batch 10—degeneration is observable, not deduced.
- Three-way verdict: Cloud reasoning leg hit this point, overturning strong form of D25 (Target Workload Priority) draft; verdict and legs in changelog.

**Owed**: None.

#### Settled Item 4: Loss origins differ between workloads; larger batches and group commit save spine-sharing type only

**Settlement**:

| | Spine-sharing type (seq) | Disjoint-spine type (multistream) |
|---|---|---|
| What is the loss | **Amortization window truncated by fsync**. Locality exists **and can be realized** | **Locality exists but cannot be realized**: operations in batch have mutually disjoint spines |
| Can larger batches save it | **Yes**. Option A/B ratio 1.54× → 1.05×, almost fully absorbed | **No**. Non-user blocks scale approximately linearly with batch (6 → 33); Option A/B ratio 3.40× → 3.71×, increasing instead |
| Can group commit save it | **Yes**, same cause as larger batch—operations accumulated in batch share same spine | **No**. Group commit saves root slots and records, while root is single; spine count not reduced by one |

⇒ What D25 (Target Workload Priority) should ask are two measurable numbers, not a stance: how many leaves one fsync carries, and how many disjoint spines those leaves land on (Settled Item 2).

**Scope**: Ratios in table compare Option A and Option B journal forms under block-count measurement basis; changing measurement basis requires recomputation (Settled Item 5); "group commit cannot save disjoint-spine type" refers to spine count, not asserting group commit provides zero benefit on that workload (root slots and records are saved as usual).

**Basis**:

- E16 (Journal Role: WAL vs Intent Log): When enlarging batches across two workload classes, Option A/B ratios converge in one and diverge in other, non-user block scaling also separated—differing loss origins between classes is measured.

**Owed**: None.

#### Settled Item 5: Workload table follows block-count measurement basis; barrier and byte measurement bases unmeasured

**Settlement**: The same gap shifts when measurement basis changes; three measurement bases yield three distinct multipliers:

| Measurement Basis | seq | multistream | Gap | Who Measured It |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Write amplification (blocks / user block, batch 1) | 1.75 | 7.00 | 4× | E16 (Journal Role: WAL vs Intent Log) |
| Barrier (times / user block, batch 1) | 0.25 | 2.0 | **8×** | **Unmeasured**, deduced |
| Ancestor bytes per fsync (node 512 B → 64 KiB) | 3.8 KiB → 167.8 KiB | Same as left | **44×**, independent of workload | **Unmeasured** |

⇒ **Block-count measurement basis systematically favors large nodes. Using numbers from E16 (Journal Role: WAL vs Intent Log) to argue fanout reverses direction.** Workload table of Settled Item 1 follows block-count measurement basis; **before using it to fix fanout or specific form of D11 (Message Buffers in Index Nodes), replenish barrier measurement basis and byte measurement basis numbers.**

**Scope**: Barrier row: this is deduction, not measurement—Option A requires 2 persistence points per fsync (root slot cannot persist before contents it points to, fsync waits for root slot before returning second point), independent of workload; inlining checksum into parent pointer per D4 (Checksum Location) creates a **computational** dependency rather than a **persistence order** dependency (ancestor content requires child checksum, but child need not land first), hence 2 rather than HEIGHT+1; until measured on real hardware it remains deduction. Byte row calculated with 40-byte pointers of E16 (Journal Role: WAL vs Intent Log) era; current pointers are 88 / 86 (D19 (Block Pointer Structure & Width Budget) Settled Item 11); byte counts un-recomputed for new width.

**Basis**:

- E16 (Journal Role: WAL vs Intent Log): Two values in block-count row measured; other two rows deduced only on its geometry—different multipliers from three measurement bases visible within same model.
- E77 (Persistence Ordering for Publish): Data integrity requires only one barrier before root slot—deduction of "2 persistence points per fsync" holds at model level; real hardware numbers unmeasured.

**Owed**: C27 (Fixing format parameters with block-count measurement basis): barrier and byte measurement bases unmeasured, deductions fixing format parameters lack a measurement basis; C26 (Barrier counts never measured).

#### Settled Item 6: Capacity measurements cannot answer tradeoffs; D25 can only be settled by humans

**Settlement**: "Single thread is not a bottleneck" and D25 (Target Workload Priority) do not ask the same question, **must not be used as its answer**.

| Measured Conclusion | Question It Answers | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Single-thread sort-merge-sweep **29.9–31.8M** entries/s (eight-round range; ≈40.8 GB/s user write), partitionable by leaf with 5.49–6.38× concurrency (16 threads) | **Whether CPU side keeps pace** | E17 (Write Buffer Merging Ceiling) |
| Multi-core encryption saturates at ≈17 GB/s with AES-NI; single-core encryption is bottleneck without AES-NI | Whether CPU side keeps pace | E6 (Cryptographic Algorithm Selection), E21 (Net Benefit of GPU Offload) |

⇒ These are **capacity** measurements (can machine handle it); D25 (Target Workload Priority) asks about **tradeoffs** (who do we optimize for). **No experiment can answer a tradeoff**—it is product judgment, settled only by humans.

**Scope**: Governs "what class of observation can serve as D25 (Target Workload Priority) answer", without denying efficacy of those capability numbers in their respective cells (supporting items of D13 (Verification Strategy), D4 (Checksum Location), D9 (Encryption) respectively, alongside verification route). Nor does it exempt experimental sources for Settled Item 1 values: those two values are measured; choosing which tier is human decision.

**Basis**:

- No experiment: Governs which class of observation cannot answer this question, containing no measurable quantity; measurable half (numbers for each workload tier) provided by Settled Item 1 basis; remaining "who to optimize for" by definition cannot be judged by experiment.
- User settlement (2026-08-29 coarse granularity decision simultaneously settled this item), verbatim words in changelog.

**Owed**: None.

#### Settled Item 7: Concurrency level for concurrent fsync has no upper bound, lower bound 16

**Settlement**: Concurrency level for concurrent fsync sets no upper bound, lower bound 16; specific value determined by disk and machine performance, not hardcoded as constant.

**Scope**: Governs single quantity of "how many concurrent fsyncs in target workload", independent of batch size or stream count (Settled Items 2, 3), nor how group commit is implemented (line of D23 (Journal Role & Format) Settled Item 1 and D16 (Publish Semantics) Settled Item 11).

**Basis**:

- User settlement 2026-09-19 (verbatim words in changelog).
- No experiment: Target value for workload, settled by user, not conclusion from experiment. Cost of measuring this target value is recorded in "**Owed**".

**Owed**: Lacks corresponding experimental numbers—E155 (Write Volume per Persistence: Three fsync Forms and Counterfactual Upper Bound) finished fourth run of segment one (2026-09-21, k axis expanded to 1024, no flip at 0% threshold), costs still unmeasured (lacking k > 1024, failure clause F11 triggered so conclusion restricted to "on k ≤ 1024"), its failure clause F11 specifically governing "any finite upper end is an arbitrary choice".
