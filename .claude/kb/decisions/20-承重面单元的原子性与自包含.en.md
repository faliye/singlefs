## D20 (Load-Bearing Surface: Unit Atomicity & Self-Containment) — Settled

[中文](20-承重面单元的原子性与自包含.md) · **English** · [日本語](20-承重面单元的原子性与自包含.ja.md)

D20 (Load-Bearing Surface: Unit Atomicity & Self-Containment) defines at which layer the load-bearing surface of this project resides, and what requirements that layer must satisfy: the **atomicity** and **self-containment** of units. It governs the status of these two axes, how atomic widths are categorized, who guarantees them, and how violations are detected. Out of scope: what specific fields are carried and their widths belongs to D18 (information carried in a block); how the two orthogonal axes of atomicity are synthesized belongs to D22 (Synthesizing Unit Atomicity); where checksums live belongs to D4 (Checksum Location); publication ordering belongs to D16 (publish semantics). Upper-layer structures—indexing forms, snapshot models, routing strategies, media layouts—can all be replaced, but the load-bearing surface cannot.

#### Settled Item 1: Load-Bearing Surface Drawn at the "Unit" Layer

**Settlement**: The load-bearing surface of this project is the **unit** layer—its **atomicity**, and its **self-containment**. Upper-layer structures (indexing forms, snapshot models, routing strategies, media layouts) can all be replaced, but the load-bearing surface cannot.

Two mutually independent reasons:

- **Extrapolatability**: Formats must live for a very long time, and the long-lived part is not whether the right tree structure was chosen at the time, but **whether the semantics of the minimum unit leaves any ambiguity**.
- **Mechanism** (independent of the above): A unit that is both self-contained and atomic makes "whole-disk scan-rebuild" physically possible—picking up any arbitrary unit can answer "who am I, whom do I belong to, and which generation am I", and it is either completely the old version or completely the new version, with no third state. **Rebuild capability is the common fallback for all upper-layer structures**: whatever upper layers are swapped for, this fallback remains intact. It is a generalization of `.claude/rules/fs-design.md` "metadata blocks must be self-describing"—generalized from "metadata blocks" to "units", and augmented with the axis of atomicity.

**Scope**: Settles which layer bears the load and what requirements that layer must satisfy, without settling any specific field or any specific synthesis mechanism. The scope of the "unit" form itself lives in `.claude/rules/fs-design.md` "on-disk space that is not a unit receives zero coverage from the core layer": on-disk space not constructed into units automatically falls outside checksums, scan-rebuild claims, checker traversals, and invariants.

**Basis**: No experiments: settles which layer the load-bearing surface is drawn at, with no quantifiable metrics; the two reasons consist of an extrapolatable judgment and a mechanistic derivation (rebuild capability requires exactly self-containment plus atomicity). User settlement, verbatim words in changelog.

**Owed**: None.

#### Settled Item 2: Self-Description Must Cover Data Blocks, Not Just Metadata Blocks

**Settlement**: The rules of I-1 (block self-description) in [invariants/01-块自描述.md](../../../crates/singlefs-checker/invariants/01-块自描述.md) refer to "any **unit**", not "any metadata block"—per settled item 1, "units" make no distinction between metadata and data. To what extent data blocks achieve this is settled by D18 (information carried in a block) settled item 3 (plaintext, and rather write larger: each data unit carries a plaintext self-describing header, a 5-tuple totaling 33 bytes; without adding `locality_id`).

**Scope**: This item only requires that "data units must also self-verify their identities", without settling field sets or widths—those reside in D18 (information carried in a block) settled items 3 / 7. Self-verifying units (journal records / root slots / system configuration slots / type 4 instance table units) are explicitly exempt from I-1.1 (block header self-stated logical address) and I-1.3 (block header tree ID consistency); their self-verification discipline has separate landing points.

**Basis**: No experiments: this item merely applies settled item 1's "no distinction between metadata and data" to the grammatical subject of the invariant; quantitative measurements live on D18 (information carried in a block) settled item 3's side. Main agent and user settlement, verbatim words in changelog.

**Owed**: None.

#### Settled Item 3: "Atomicity" Must Be Formulated as Three Decidable Questions

**Settlement**: "Atomicity" must not be used as an adjective; it must answer at least three questions, and lacking any single one means D20 (Load-Bearing Surface: Unit Atomicity & Self-Containment) has not yet landed:

| Question | Where answered |
|---|---|
| 1. What the atomic width is | Settled item 4 (specified separately by unit class) and settled item 7 (device-side is 512 and cannot be relied upon) |
| 2. Who guarantees it | Settled item 4 (units with parent pointers rely on parent pointer checksums + publication order; self-verifying units rely on core layer synthesis) |
| 3. How violations are detected | Settled item 6 (crash-point replay + checker) |

**Scope**: The three questions form D20 (Load-Bearing Surface: Unit Atomicity & Self-Containment)'s own landing checklist; checks for Question 3 do not yet have a code object (settled item 6); having answered the three questions does not equal having verified the three questions; "how to synthesize" is not answered here, as those two orthogonal axes live in D22 (Synthesizing Unit Atomicity) settled items 19 / 20 / 21.

**Basis**: No experiments: settles which questions D20 (Load-Bearing Surface: Unit Atomicity & Self-Containment) must answer, with no quantifiable metrics; it represents the form taken on the load-bearing surface by the discipline "never substitute adjectives for criteria". Main agent settlement.

**Owed**: C16 (pipeline declares failure model exception).

#### Settled Item 4: Atomic Width Specified Separately by Unit Class; Combining into One Statement Is Erroneous

**Settlement**: The answers to the two questions must be given separately by unit class.

| Unit Class | Question 1: What the atomic width is | Question 2: Who guarantees it |
|---|---|---|
| **Units with parent pointers** (majority) | **Does not depend on any width.** Whether tearing granularity is 512, 4 KiB, or full block has zero impact on the parent pointer checksum argument (D22 (Synthesizing Unit Atomicity) settled item 19 / settled item 20) | Core layer, using D4 (Checksum Location) parent-pointer-inlined checksum + D16 (publish semantics) publication ordering |
| **Self-verifying units** (root slots, journal record headers, **system configuration slots**) | **Equals runtime-probed `physical_block_size`, hard-coding forbidden.** This statement describes the **resolution for judging tearing**, not the structural dimension. See explicit prohibition in D23 (Role and Format of Journal) settled item 4 | Core layer synthesis (whole-unit checksum + generation number + slot rotation, see D22 (Synthesizing Unit Atomicity) settled item 21); device declarations serve only as upper-bound parameters |

**The statement that "atomic width is a format constant" is erroneous**; it compresses two separate cells into one sentence: the majority of units have no need to know the width at all; while the small cell that does need to know is explicitly forbidden from welding numbers into the format.

**Whether device declarations are usable splits into two cases, not a single blanket statement** (verified on-the-spot in `drivers/nvme/host/core.c` via `nvme_configure_atomic_write()`):

```c
if ((id->nsfeat & NVME_NS_FEAT_ATOMICS) && id->nawupf) {
        atomic_bs = (1 + le16_to_cpu(id->nawupf)) * bs;
```

| Case | Distinguishable? | Usable? |
|---|---|---|
| No ATOMICS feature bit, or `nawupf == 0` | **Indistinguishable**—`atomic_bs` falls back to logical block, isomorphic to "nothing declared" | Cannot |
| Has ATOMICS bit and `nawupf > 0` | **Strictly distinguishable**, `atomic_bs = (1+nawupf) × bs > bs` | Can, but **only as an upper-bound parameter** |

**Scope**: The row for self-verifying units states the **resolution for judging tearing**, not the **structural dimension**—the structural dimension of system configuration slots is a format constant of 4096 (registered name `SYSTEM_CONFIGURATION_SLOT_BYTES`, D22 (Synthesizing Unit Atomicity) settled item 2), whereas root slot width is what "equals mount-time probed `physical_block_size`". Obtaining a credible upper bound buys no new safety: detection mechanisms themselves do not depend on width; it affects only the alignment cost of the self-verifying unit cell. ⚠️ It does not resolve C212 (fixed structure write smaller than io_min): on devices where `io_min` is greater than `physical_block_size`, writing a system configuration slot remains a write smaller than `io_min`, and the conflict with D2 (RAID Stripe Policy) settled item 16 remains unchanged.

**Basis**: No experiments: answers to both questions are derived from "whether the parent pointer checksum argument needs to know the width", with no quantifiable metrics; the two cases of device declarations were confirmed line-by-line in kernel source code, sourced from `drivers/nvme/host/core.c` via `nvme_configure_atomic_write()`, not verified in this project. The enumeration of system configuration slots into this cell was a user settlement (the rule itself did not change a single word), verbatim words in changelog.

**Owed**: C212 (fixed structure write smaller than io_min).

#### Settled Item 5: What the Core Layer Synthesizes—Seven Mechanisms with Their Respective Blind Spots Explicitly Stated

**Settlement**:

| Mechanism | Can detect | Cannot detect |
|---|---|---|
| Parent-pointer-inlined checksum (D4 (Checksum Location)) + publication ordering (D16 (publish semantics)) | Arbitrary granularity tearing, ghost writes, bit flips, child block lost writes | Parent itself losing writes (recurses to grandparent); root |
| Whole-unit checksum | Unit bytes not self-consistent | **Complete stale units**; false negatives based on bit width |
| Generation number / `jsn` actively checked | Complete stale units; sequence gaps | **Head landed but tail failed**—number in header is new |
| Slot rotation + verify all before selecting newest | Tearing of the sole replica loses witness | Rollback depth (bounded by block reuse delay K) |
| Per-sector stamping (D22 (Synthesizing Unit Atomicity) Axis ②, currently settled as not done) | Stale sectors **deterministically** detected; tearing **location** | Sector content corruption; entire record is stale |
| In-flight record count upper bound | Judges checksum failures outside window as corruption rather than tearing | —— |
| Write side never issues writes smaller than mapping unit | **Prevents** device-internal RMW from corrupting neighbors, does not detect anything | —— |

⚠️ **Whole-unit checksum and generation number alone are not closed, must be paired**: the former misses stale units, while the latter misses head/tail misalignment.

**Scope**: This table enumerates mechanisms and blind spots, without judging which one should be executed—whether per-sector stamping is done resides in D22 (Synthesizing Unit Atomicity) settled item 21 (currently settled as not done). When D22 (Synthesizing Unit Atomicity) rejected it, it compared only the single dimension of miss rate, whereas stamping also buys **alignment exemption** (when verdict resolution is sector-level, multiple records squeezed into the same atomic unit can still have tearing judged, whereas under whole-unit checksum none beyond the first can be judged); the fact that the comparison set for that rejection reason was incomplete has already been recorded in D22 (Synthesizing Unit Atomicity) settled item 21; whether to reopen belongs to D22 (Synthesizing Unit Atomicity), and this item merely tracks the accounting.

**Basis**:

- E23 (journal geometry): the third benefit bought by per-sector stamping is alignment exemption, whose alignment cost on 4Kn is 29.3x—benefits of that row in the seven mechanisms table extend well beyond the single dimension of miss rate.
- Three-party debate: forward-proving leg split "atomic width" into device-declared width and judgment width and provided the table of blind spots for the seven mechanisms; two non-forward legs converged independently on device declarations; main agent adopted and narrowed after line-by-line examination of kernel source code; process in changelog.

**Owed**: C16 (pipeline declares failure model exception).

#### Settled Item 6: How Violations Are Detected—Crash-Point Replay + Checker

**Settlement**: The landing point for Question 3 is **crash-point replay + checker**. Both are present (gate checker-tier-crash-point-replay, `crates/singlefs-checker`), but crash-point replay currently records only this project's own single pipeline, enumerating whole writes and conflating tearing into "unpersisted" (D13 (verification route) settled item 4) => "failure atomicity width / tearing / reordering" as declared per pipeline still has **zero coverage**.

**Scope**: Torn writes cannot be induced on the local host either (`dm-flakey` has only `drop_writes` / `error_writes` / `corrupt_bio_byte`) => this question must await the second pipeline and torn write injection before it can be verified. "Landing point settled" does not equal "already verified".

**Basis**: No experiments: settles which two harnesses are entrusted with this question, with no quantifiable metrics; the discriminative power of both harnesses lives on D13 (verification route)'s side, and injection tools for the tearing category do not yet exist. Main agent settlement.

**Owed**: C16 (pipeline declares failure model exception).

#### Settled Item 7: Device-Side Atomic Width Is 512 and Cannot Be Relied Upon

**Settlement**: **This project cannot assume an atomicity larger than 512 bytes, and even 512 can only be treated as "holding with high probability". Unit atomicity must be synthesized in-house.**

Five facts confirmed by word-by-word inspection:

| # | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Local NVMe: `atomic_write_unit_min = atomic_write_unit_max = logical_block_size = physical_block_size = 512` | Read live from `/sys/block/nvme0n1/queue/` |
| 2 | **On Linux, `physical_block_size` is already "the power-fail atomic width promised by the device"** | `drivers/nvme/host/core.c`: `lim->physical_block_size = min(phys_bs, atomic_bs);` preceded by verbatim comment: "Linux filesystems assume writing a single physical block is an atomic operation. Hence limit the physical block size to the value of the Atomic Write Unit **Power Fail** parameter." |
| 3 | Kernel only recognizes Power Fail variants (`AWUPF`/`NAWUPF`), with **zero references** to `AWUN`/`NAWUN` | `nvme_configure_atomic_write()` |
| 4 | **"Device declaring 1 block" is indistinguishable from "device declaring nothing"** | `drivers/nvme/host/core.c` in `nvme_configure_atomic_write()`: when device declares nothing it executes `atomic_bs = (1 + ns->ctrl->subsys->awupf) * bs`, `awupf == 0` => `atomic_bs = bs`, and `lim->features \|= BLK_FEAT_ATOMIC_WRITES` **executes unconditionally**, with no branch saying "do not set feature if undeclared" |
| 5 | **Block layer has never promised that "a bio will not be torn"** | The four `atomic_write_*` entries in `Documentation/ABI/stable/sysfs-block` contain **not a single occurrence** of crash / power loss; `Documentation/block/writeback_cache_control.rst` (95 lines) **contains zero mentions of atomic / torn / tear throughout**—`REQ_PREFLUSH`/`REQ_FUA` govern persistence ordering |

**Counter-evidence from production implementations—all synthesize tear detection in-house, none relies on the device** (external implementations serve only as counter-evidence and mechanisms, not verified in this project):

| Implementation | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **ZFS root** | 4 labels × 128K uberblock ring; slot number = `ub_txg % (VDEV_UBERBLOCK_COUNT − m)` (**rotation, never overwrites previous generation**; `m` is slot count consumed by multi-host protection MMP); SHA-256 **inlined at block tail**, verifier is block's own physical offset; reads sweep all slots selecting newest via `txg → timestamp`; even labels → uberblock → odd labels three stages with flushes |
| **ZFS regular blocks** | Checksums in parent pointer; tearing => mismatch => repaired via replicas |
| **btrfs nodes** | `csum` covers `[32, nodesize)`; **second independent gate**: parent pointer transid and node header generation differential check; blocks released within current transaction are **pinned and unreusable** |
| **XFS log** | `xlog_pack_data()`: **first `__be32` of each 512-byte slot overwritten with cycle_lsn, original value stored in record header `h_cycle_data[i]`**, restored during recovery; additionally whole-record CRC. Verbatim quote: "Storage without sector atomicity guarantees can result in torn writes… **Our only means to detect this scenario is via CRC verification.**" |
| **jbd2** | Commit block whole-block csum + `REQ_PREFLUSH\|REQ_FUA` ordering; additionally a `verify_partial` accepting "valid header only, remainder zero" |

Two exceptions, both pointing toward the same conclusion: the only explicit claim of device reliance is ZFS `vdev_label.c` stating "the disk does not do single-sector overwrites atomically (**even though it is required to**)"—the scale is **single sector 512**, and ZFS still bypasses it via **slot switching**; the only true consumer of device >512 atomic guarantees is XFS `RWF_ATOMIC` direct write for **user data**, backed by a pure software copy-on-write fallback (`-ENOPROTOOPT` => switch to COW path), while **not a single byte of XFS's own logs or metadata takes this path**.

**Scope**: ⚠️ **"Newer devices" does not equal "larger atomic write width".** NVMe atomic write units depend on what the device itself declares for `AWUN` / `NAWUPF`; absence of declaration means absence of capability. Abandoning compatibility with legacy devices buys "can assume 4 KiB physical sectors", "can assume FLUSH/FUA synthesized by block layer", "can assume concurrent commits"—**it does not buy atomic write width**. The quote from ZFS `vdev_label.c` was recorded earlier in D20 (Load-Bearing Surface: Unit Atomicity & Self-Containment); no OpenZFS source on local host, cannot be independently verified.

**Basis**: No experiments: five facts are observations obtained from line-by-line inspection of kernel source and sysfs, not numbers produced in this project; the table of production implementations serves strictly as counter-evidence and mechanisms per `.claude/singlefs-ai-sop/rules/evidence-discipline.md` "how other projects do things is a clue, not evidence"—it shifts the burden of proof to the "rely on device" side, without constituting affirmative evidence. Main agent settlement, two independent paths plus self-check, process in changelog.

**Owed**: C16 (pipeline declares failure model exception).
