## D3 Space Allocation —— Settled

[中文](03-空间分配.md) · **English** · [日本語](03-領域割り当て.ja.md)

D3 (Space Allocation) governs the allocator aspect: whether space is classified, what form reservations take, granularity and vehicle and on-disk encoding of allocation records, placement of user data and commit-endogenous blocks, geometry of clustered segments, the unique registry position of allocation acceptance conjunctions, and the hard requirement that "false ENOSPC is prohibited, and deleted space must be usable within bounded steps." What it does not govern: authoritative form of the acceptance inequality and in-flight synthesis of acceptance are moved to D28 (Mount-Time Commitments) settled items 1 and 2, with settled items 4 and 6 keeping only pointers without renumbering; trigger criteria, termination predicates, and resident floor for compaction are in D26 (Background Compaction and Placement Reclamation); which devices a single write lands on is in D2 (RAID Stripe Policy) settled item 8; what `df` reports and the gate for advancing F are in D16 (Publish Semantics) settled item 1; snapshot-side deadlist discipline is in D5 (Snapshot / Space Accounting Mechanism).

#### Settled Item 1: Allocation Records Answer "Belongs to Active Version" Per-Placement

**Settlement**: Allocation records are the authoritative recording point for the predicate "whether a placement belongs to the active version at this moment," not an aggregate quantity—the accounting tree in D5 (Snapshot / Space Accounting Mechanism) settled item 3 holds "(statistic, dimension tuple, generation) → full value" and cannot answer for an individual placement. The predicate is synthesized from two halves, both settled:

| Half | Provided by | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Has this placement been allocated | Allocation records of **D3 (Space Allocation)** | Free placement belongs to no version |
| Does the allocated placement belong to the **active** version | Deadlist of **D5 (Snapshot / Space Accounting Mechanism)** | Placement entering deadlist is referenced only by snapshots, not in live tree |

"Authoritative recording point" is a runtime measurement basis, not an ontological state category: allocation records **are derived state**, and the entire tree can be reconstructed from units + accounting + roots; authoritative state remains those three without expansion (authoritative records in D21 (Boundary Between Authoritative and Derived States) settled item 5); runtime evaluation of this predicate may only query it without ad-hoc scanning, while if lost it can be reconstructed.

**Scope**: Governs runtime per-placement evaluation of active version. Does not govern active version evaluation of multi-version units during scan rebuild—that cell is settled per C113 (No Input to Current-Version Determination of Multi-Version Units in Scan Rebuild) as "unit write sequence + instance table published predicate + ancestor table", without reading this predicate; recalculation source in D11 (Message Buffers in Index Nodes) settled item 4 therefore excludes allocation records. Compaction **policy** itself is outside scope (items in D26 (Background Compaction and Placement Reclamation)); what was measured is how much overhead the carrier forcibly incurs.

**Basis**:

- E59 (Can Buffered Messages Recompute Solely from Units): Scanning only units cannot distinguish active values across multiple readable copies of the same key (the indistinguishable count exactly equals overwrite count); adding this predicate eliminates divergences across all four message categories—this is what the per-placement half buys.
- E74 (Allocation Record Entry Count and Compaction Cost): Per-placement entry count scales with capacity, differing in dimension from the accounting tree; neither tree was built incorrectly. Compaction path has no terms scaling with disk capacity, while COW rewrites entire nodes, creating hundreds-fold difference between contiguous and scattered keys—key in settled item 3 places compaction on favorable end.
- User settlement, original wording in history.

**Owed check**: C122 (entry count measurement basis after allocation record point-delete); C412 (wall-clock cost of compaction unmeasured).

#### Settled Item 2: Tombstones and livelist FREE Entries Counted into Worst Case by Acceptance Control

**Settlement**: Deletion operation itself requires disk writes—D18 (Information Carried in a Block) settled item 8 settles that "deletion requires writing tombstone units", and deleting blocks in a writable head appends a FREE entry (36 bytes, D6 (Snapshot Implementation Model) settled item 2) to livelist, while settled item 17 principle verbatim states "COW file system must guarantee that 'releasing space' operation itself does not require requesting space". Solution is not carving exceptions for deletion path, but collecting this cost in advance at write time: acceptance control in settled item 15 already mandates that "any operation increasing space usage must calculate **how much is needed to unwind itself in worst case** before admission"—**tombstones are part of 'unwinding itself'** and counted into that amount; livelist FREE entries are likewise part of "unwinding itself", counted and prepaid at write time. Tombstones are accounted per object: amortized share of one tombstone record per live object (nlink ≥ 1, including internal nodes of tombstone tree) counted into committed allowance (D5 (Snapshot / Space Accounting Mechanism) settled item 4 item 6, accounting label 6), plus resident slots pool-wide. Exact formula: label 6 = replica_count × ((2 + H_tombstone + 1 + 2 + H_retire + 1) × 16384 + 41 × n_live + 35 × n_records), in bytes, recorded as sum across replicas (C375 (Pending deletion occupancy and committed reserve recorded by copies or sum lacks clause)); H_tombstone is internal height of active tombstone tree, H_retire is internal height of retirement tree (both 0 for null root or root-only), read live from code 2 headers of respective tree roots (precedent: ckpt_cost reading in D28 (Mount-Time Commitments) settled item 4); 41 = ⌈2 × 16384 ÷ 815⌉, rounded up per replica per object then multiplied by n_live; n_live = count of records with nlink ≥ 1 in active inode tree; 35 = ⌈32768 ÷ 959⌉, n_records = total record count in active inode tree (including nlink = 0): retirement entries n_records does not count n_live because retirement records are written in final batch, where object between intention and final batch is no longer in n_live, and attaching retirement share to n_live would release it before use; for versions without `deleted_inodes` entry in tree table, label 6 is 0. New writes in deletion batches covered by resident deduction in acceptance formula "ring window = (root ring slot count 3 × S + 1) × batch upper bound W" (D28 (Mount-Time Commitments) settled item 1, S is system configuration slots per region), not entering accounting rows. Deletion-class publishes (intention, batches, continuation batches in mount) skip formula check while still evaluating placement check; write-class publishes evaluated against post-publish label; ring window counted from first file version publish. ⇒ Deletion path still does not request space: settled item 17 principle unchanged verbatim; what changed is algorithm for "how much needed to unwind itself".

**Scope**: Settles who pays this charge (acceptance control, paid at write time), not how much—version 1 writes one 40-byte full object tombstone per unlink (D18 (Information Carried in a Block) settled item 24); whether batch amortization is viable and whether deletion works under full disk unquantified; deletion path implemented (`crates/singlefs-core/src/unlink.rs`), awaiting stage 2 of E168 (Ring Window for Borrowing Reserve Capacity on Deletion) on real deletion batches (stage 1 ran on patched replica 2026-10-03, stage 2 unrun). Tightens settled item 15 "reservation dynamically tracks real demand without idle holding": reservation bound to "whether it will be deleted in future"; tombstone term is pure waste for never-deleted objects, with waste unmeasured. Deletion-class publish skipping formula survived one attack round; write-class publish checking post-publish label and ring window from first file version survived zero rounds of attack; resident slot formula 2 + H_tombstone + 1 read live survived zero rounds of attack; retire term (2 + H_retire + 1, 35 × n_records) combined with retired container term in D28 (Mount-Time Commitments) settled item 4 survived zero rounds of attack; under "delete → rollback" cycle their combination still oversells, recorded in C617 (tombstones left by rollback after deletion are not covered by prepayment).

**Basis**:

- E82 (In-Flight Synthesis for Admission): Forgetting tombstone charge deadlocks all deletions on full disk—"unwinding itself" containing tombstones is executable, not rhetoric.
- E130 (Does Per-Head Livelist Pass Bounded Destruction): Extra bytes written per released block elevate livelist append to part of "unwinding itself"; failing to prepay conflicts with settled item 17 principle.
- User settlement (tombstone half and later livelist half), original wording in history.
- Tombstone amortized per live object plus pool-wide resident copy, deletion-class publish skipping formula, write-class publish checking post-publish label, ring window starting from first file version: `research/prompts/m3-delete-r3-main-verification.md` section 3 rows G1-tombstone prepayment, G1-deletion-class publish judgment, G1-write-class publish, G1-window starting point.

#### Settled Item 5: Commit-Endogenous Blocks Allocated from Clustered Segments

**Settlement**: Blocks generated by commit itself—COW new nodes of allocation record tree, accounting tree, and other structures—must be allocated from clustered segments (bump segments) contiguous in key space; user data block placement not subject to this constraint. Segment geometry and bump order in settled item 10; fallback on exhaustion in settled item 8 item 2. Commit-endogenous blocks failing to find empty slots during fixed-point allocation (`NoFreeSlotOnAnyDevice`) reject this publish prior to any disk write, leaving disk unchanged; cell covers splits and clustered segment tiers uncovered by acceptance formula (C545 (space admission does not cover splits and the cluster segment layer)).

**Scope**: Represents **allocator policy not format bit**—clustered segment position and length are runtime choices, undeclared by any on-disk field; implementations violating it produce fully legal images, albeit hundreds of times costlier per publish. ⇒ Restrained solely by runtime readings: commit path records "fixed-point convergence iterations for current publish", failing red if exceeding declared bound (`.claude/rules/fs-design.md` hard requirement 4: wrong branch is performance cliff, cliffs do not return errors). Whether clustered segments across devices align when w ≥ 2 (each unit's w copies landing in single column ⇒ w clustered segments advancing independently) lacks repo clauses, left to implementation of this item.

**Basis**:

- E148 (Commit Fixed Point Recomputed with Two Record Trees): Clustered commit-endogenous blocks under two record trees yield constant fixed point; fully scattered exhibits no bound—constant bound exists solely under clustered policy.
- E81 (Fixed Point of Commits): All accounting structures are COW trees, writing nodes generates allocations, and fixed-point amplification depends on whether commit-endogenous blocks cluster—fully scattered fsync drags metadata blocks leaving I-8.1 (Ring Geometry Sufficiently Large) "worst transaction usage" without usable upper bound; clustering commit-endogenous blocks breaks chain, unaffected by fully random user data. Yields lower bound (assuming one-shot COW at publish end); interleave-as-you-insert implementations costlier.

**Settlement**: Placement granularity takes 16384 bytes (one 16 KiB slot); single record covers one storage unit. key = (device identity 4, 16 KiB slot number 6) totaling 10 bytes; value = (span segment 2 containing released flag, allocation generation or release generation 8) totaling 10 bytes, generation storing full checkpoint number.

**Entries are not deleted, not point-deleted upon placement release** (C113 (No Input to Current-Version Determination of Multi-Version Units in Scan Rebuild) settlement P4): rewritten as "released + release generation"—release generation written into those 8 bytes, released flag borrows highest bit of span segment, entry retained until overwritten upon placement reallocation. Reason against point deletion: tree sorted by placement with generation in value; point deletion by generation requires full-tree scan, while generation list needed for point deletion is precisely the memory state lost on crash; entry rewrite and COW producing this release take effect atomically in same publish—allocation records are COW tree under root (D22 (Synthesizing Unit Atomicity) settled item 3), entries published with root, release generation = checkpoint_txg of that publish; crash discards unpublished checkpoint, leaving zero traces of releases therein while referenced older versions remain referenced by selected root. Memory state of defer queue lost on crash, handled by gate "release generation ≤ max(F_effective, oldest valid root in ring)" (I-7.4 (Blocks in Recent K Generations Not Reused) note; predicate supplied by D16 (Publish Semantics) settled item 1, validity evaluated by instance table; mechanism to advance F depends on this predicate). "Allocation generation" measurement basis: relocation destination record writes unit birth generation, not relocation publish txg (D26 (Background Compaction and Placement Reclamation) settled item 5).

**"Release" measurement basis** = instant allocator places placement into defer queue = referenced by no root (including snapshots); placements referenced by snapshots enter deadlist rather than defer per D5 (Snapshot / Space Accounting Mechanism) deadlist discipline, released only when snapshot destroyed ⇒ release generation is later than publish where last referencing root was destroyed. #### Settled Item 8: User Data Blocks Do Not Enter Clustered Segments; Placement Is Smallest Starting Free Slot in Selected Device

**Settlement**: Five rules.

1. **Version 1 user data blocks do not enter clustered segments. Placement**: After device set selected by D2 (RAID Stripe Policy) settled item 8, each selected device takes the **placement with smallest starting slot number, 32768-aligned start, both slots empty in span, not in any open clustered segment, and full span passing allocation acceptance conjunctions (seven clauses, registered solely in settled item 7)**; **no device selection, no device round-robin** (device dimension belongs to D2 (RAID Stripe Policy) settled item 8); `R` in protection window (D26 (Background Compaction and Placement Reclamation) settled item 1 item 3) is already one of the seven conjunctions, not separately listed.
2. **Clustered segments closed to user data during mount**: Clustered segments opened during this mount (currently open segment and segments opened prior to fallback) are closed to user data (settled item 10 ② "any open clustered segment" refers to these); after reopen (bump pointer and "segments opened during this mount" reside solely in memory, opening fresh segment upon mount, settled item 10 ①), segments opened in previous mount holding commit-endogenous blocks open to user data, mixed placement permitted. Settled item 5 "placement of user data blocks not subject to this constraint" holds as-is without generalization; commit-endogenous blocks falling back on segment exhaustion land in smallest slot number free slot on device, likewise excluding `R`—C146 (fallback policy with no empty segment undefined repo-wide) item ② (where fallback lands) receives policy, while item ① (compaction next destination falling back to segment currently being drained) belongs to D26 (Background Compaction and Placement Reclamation) settled item 1.
3. **Placements for ≤ 4 KiB tier prior to packing follow rule 1**; containers written by D27 (Small-Data Packing Container) settled item 6 packing also follow rule 1.
4. **Restricted to version 1**.
