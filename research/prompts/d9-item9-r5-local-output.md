1. Two keyless mounts: Step 1: Volume locked, lease area signed (slots 1-10). Step 2: First keyless mount writes to slot 5. Step 3: Crash occurs. Step 4: Second keyless mount starts, unaware of slot 5 usage, writes to slot 5. Step 5: Unlock occurs. Key side replays ring: first mount's write to 5, then second mount's write to 5. Key side accepts both batches. Data from first mount is overwritten and lost. Rule broken: lease area allows overwriting without tracking prior writes. Smallest change: lease area must track used slots via a live map updated by keyless mounts, signed by key side during unlock.

2. Lease area slots are allocated in admission rule calculation (part of "allocated" term). Before locking, free space report includes lease area slots as available. After locking, they are reserved. At unlock, unused lease slots must be returned to available space. Crash during unlock after processing ring but before returning unused slots: lease area slots remain allocated. User sees less free space than actual. Rule broken: allocation update for unused lease slots is not atomic. Smallest change: return unused lease slots atomically during unlock, ensuring crash during unlock does not leave them allocated.

3. Observer sees slot X checksum changed between lock periods. Slot X is live in both periods. Encryption uses random IVs per write. Same plaintext can produce different ciphertexts due to IV changes. Therefore, observer cannot deduce if plaintext contents changed. Step 1: Checksum of slot X changed. Step 2: Slot X is live in both periods. Step 3: Encryption uses IVs. Step 4: Ciphertext change does not imply plaintext change. What it needs: knowledge that IVs are used in encryption (standard practice). No inference about plaintext contents possible.
