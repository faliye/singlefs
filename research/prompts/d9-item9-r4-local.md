You are reviewing a design for an encrypted copy-on-write filesystem. Your stance is: find a concrete counterexample. Do not summarize, do not praise. Do not use any markdown emphasis such as bold or italics. Answer in English. Answer all three items, number them 1, 2, 3. Keep each item under 250 words.

Setting, all settled and not open for debate. The whole volume is encrypted and mounting requires the key. A mount without the key must still be able to move blocks, because continuous background defragmentation is a hard promise. It may move blocks but may never free them, and it may only write inside a lease area signed by the key side before locking. Every pointer holds one location entry per replica and each entry carries a keyless corruption checksum. The replica width floor is two. Data unit identities are plaintext in the unit header, metadata unit identities are ciphertext. An attacker who reorders blocks is already accepted as undetectable, so attacks are out of scope for this review, only media faults, staleness and crashes are in scope.

The design under review. At lock time the key side signs three read only tables keyed by physical slot, covering only the slots in the lease area plus the slots it plans to move this period, not the whole pool: a three valued live slot table saying dead, live movable, or live pinned; a table from slot to the ciphertext checksum of what lives there; and a snapshot of stripe membership. Each four kibibyte page of these tables carries its own signature, and separately a keyless CRC32C so the keyless mount can detect media faults on its own. The keyless mount moves units inside the lease area and appends one record per move to a ring. At unlock the key side verifies each page signature, replays the ring, and only then rewrites the authoritative pointer and retires the source slot. Source slots are never overwritten during the lock period. The claim being defended is that a corrupted or stale plaintext table can only cause wasted work, never data loss and never a silently wrong result.

Your task:

1. Break the claim without an attacker. Construct a history in which one of the three tables is stale, not corrupt, its CRC32C passes, and the keyless mount acting on it produces data loss or a silently wrong result rather than wasted work. Remember it cannot free anything and cannot overwrite a source slot.

2. One page of the tables fails its signature at unlock, so the moves it covered are not accepted. The source slots still hold their data, but the destination slots inside the lease area have been written. Say what those destination slots are afterwards: are they leaked space, reclaimable space, or something else, and name which party knows they exist. Give a history where nobody can ever reclaim them.

3. The stripe membership snapshot is the only input the keyless mount has for rebuilding a lost column of a three column stripe, because the authoritative membership record is encrypted. Construct a history during a lock period in which acting on that snapshot writes a wrong rebuilt column, and say at what moment the wrongness becomes detectable and by whom.
