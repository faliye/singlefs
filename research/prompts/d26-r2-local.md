You are reviewing a design for a copy-on-write filesystem. Your stance is: find a concrete counterexample. Do not summarize, do not praise. Do not use any markdown emphasis such as bold or italics. Answer in English. Answer all three items, number them 1, 2, 3. Keep each item under 250 words.

Setting, all settled and not open for debate. Background defragmentation is a resident, automatic, hard promise. It is a background, resumable, non decision path, so it is allowed to traverse and allowed to be slow. Any operation that may touch an unbounded number of extents must first write an intent record and then proceed in batches; the intent must be idempotent, must state a target state and never an increment, and every intermediate state must itself be a legal filesystem state. The admission rule for space is: available equals, summed over devices, capacity minus allocated minus unreclaimable minus deferred, then minus pending deletion and minus already promised reservation. A separate rule says that freeing space must never itself require allocating space. Three measured quantities are persisted in the accounting tree and are available immediately after mount: a fragmentation ratio defined as runs divided by length, a count of fully empty cluster segments, and a count of unreclaimable bytes. A measurement model has already shown that without defragmentation the fragmentation ratio saturates at its ceiling after a few hundred rounds, and that rewriting neighbours has a ceiling of its own while defragmentation improves as its budget grows.

The design under review. Defragmentation starts when any one of three watermarks is crossed: too few empty cluster segments, fragmentation ratio above a high threshold, or unreclaimable bytes above a fraction of capacity. It stops only when all three have returned past their low thresholds. Thresholds are runtime tunables, not format constants. Its intent record states a target state of the form clear region R. It requests space through the ordinary admission rule, counting its worst case batch as promised reservation, and if the pool is full it simply stalls.

Then say how large a permanent reserve would have to be to prevent it, and which term of the admission rule that reserve belongs to.
