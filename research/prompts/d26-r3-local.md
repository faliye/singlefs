You are reviewing a design for a copy-on-write filesystem. Your stance is: find a concrete counterexample. Do not summarize, do not praise. Do not use any markdown emphasis such as bold or italics. Answer in English. Answer all three items, number them 1, 2, 3. Keep each item under 250 words.

Setting, all settled and not open for debate. Background defragmentation is resident, automatic, and a hard promise. Any operation touching an unbounded number of extents must first write an intent record stating a target state, never an increment, and then proceed in batches, resuming after a crash. Allocation records live in a tree keyed by physical location; each entry carries the generation in which the location was allocated or freed, plus one bit saying it has been freed. Blocks produced by the commit itself must be allocated from a contiguous cluster segment; a measurement showed that when they are scattered instead, one commit of twelve units drags out roughly five thousand nine hundred metadata blocks and the worst case transaction size has no usable upper bound. Another measurement showed that cluster segments are not self sustaining: without defragmentation, over ninety percent of allocations fall back out of the cluster segment, and defragmentation budget is the only force that lowers that.

The design under review. Defragmentation runs continuously; its budget is adjusted by three watermarks, and a single batch stops when its marginal benefit, the reduction in fragmentation divided by the bytes written, falls below a threshold. Its intent record states the target: within region R there exists no location whose allocation generation is less than or equal to c0 and which has not been freed, where c0 is the generation at the moment the intent was written. Ordinary foreground writes are never blocked from allocating inside R. The count of bytes moved this round is a runtime counter only; it is not persisted, and losing it is declared harmless.

Your task:

2. The target state is monotone, since foreground writes into R carry generations above c0 and therefore cannot violate it.
