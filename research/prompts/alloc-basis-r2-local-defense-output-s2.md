1a The attack states that under SERIAL, space that has been released but is not yet reclaimable does not count toward available space at gate 1, causing requests that require raising F to be rejected immediately at gate 1 without entering gate 2. Fact F2 establishes that gate 1's available calculation subtracts defer_pending_release unconditionally, as it is part of the nine-term formula. Fact F1 establishes the ordering: gate 1 (D28's available >= requested) is checked first, and if it fails, the request is rejected outright with gate 2 never entered. Refuted if: gate 1's available calculation does not subtract defer_pending_release unconditionally, or gate 2 is entered before gate 1.

1b Under SERIAL, df equals F2's full output (the nine-term formula), as F5 states "available already subtracts this item; df reports available" and D3 item 9 clause 1 requires df to match the available used for admission checks. F2 defines available as the nine-term formula, F3 confirms "allocated" and "defer_pending_release" in F2 are maintained statistics, and F6 clause 1 states "whenever df reports free space >= s, writing s bytes must succeed." If a request needs raising F to succeed, gate 1 (F2's available) is less than s (since unreclaimable space is subtracted), so df < s. F6 clause 1 explicitly permits ENOSPC when df < s, so this is not a false ENOSPC. Refuted if: df does not equal F2's full output, or F6 clause 1 does not permit ENOSPC when df < s.

1c Yes, gate 2 can be triggered by another admission attempt that passes gate 1 (i.e., gate 1 available >= requested) but fails the allocatable check in D16 item 1's admission row. F4 states "when admission is insufficient, first push empty publishes to raise F" — admission insufficiency here refers to gate 2's allocatable check failing after gate 1 passes. Such a request triggers raise F pushes (F4), which update statistics via F8's reclaim_released_up_to (called when F is raised). This reduces defer_pending_release (statistic item 5), increasing gate 1's available for subsequent requests. The bound of 3 user-visible publishes (F6 clause 2) is satisfied because filesystem pushes for raising F do not count toward the bound. Refuted if: gate 2 cannot be triggered by an admission attempt that passes gate 1, or F8's reclaim mechanism does not update defer_pending_release when F is raised.

1d The E139 excess in F6's warning shows the adopted mechanism (D16 item 1) also fails the literal bound of 3 publishes, as stated: "E139 has cells that exceed it... those instances are real ENOSPC, but D3 item 9 clause 2, judged literally, does not meet the bound." F4's skip of raise F push before the write row publish does not alter this, as it only affects metadata in the first publish. This fact does not weaken SERIAL specifically, since the adopted mechanism already violates the bound, making SERIAL's potential failure consistent with current practice. Refuted if: E139 does not exceed the bound of 3 publishes under the adopted mechanism, or F4's skip of raise F push prevents the bound from being violated.

1e DOES NOT HOLD. F6's warning explicitly states E139 exceeds the bound of 3 publishes for user-visible state changes, and "D3 item 9 clause 2, judged literally, does not meet the bound." This fact directly blocks the escape, as the requirement "must succeed within a bounded, computable number of steps" is violated in practice under the adopted mechanism, and no fact shows SERIAL avoids this violation. Refuted if: E139 does not exceed the bound of 3 publishes, or F6's warning does not state the bound is violated.
