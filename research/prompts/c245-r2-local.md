How a copy-on-write file system's crash recovery should find the first journal record to replay after the chosen root, when the record that the root covers may be overwritten or unreadable.

Answer in English. Do not use any markdown emphasis: no bold, no italics, no headings with asterisks. Use plain numbered answers.

Facts from the design (treat them as given; F10 is an observation of the implementation, not a rule):

F1. The journal is a fixed-size ring of 4 KiB record slots on two mirrored disks. Record n lives at ring offset ((counter - 1) mod slot_count) * 4096, where counter is a 48-bit number. Each record carries its instance id (32 bits) and counter (48 bits); together they are the record's sequence number.
F2. Every publish increments checkpoint_txg by one, and a publish triggered by fsync is also a publish. One publish may write more than one record; one record holds one transaction, and each record carries the checkpoint_txg of its publish (this last part is the main agent's reading).
F3. Each record carries previous_hash = CRC32C of the header of the logically previous record written by the same instance; the first record an instance writes has previous_hash 0. The write path never reads the disk to compute it.
F4. Recovery scans the whole ring, validates every record, chooses the newest readable root (instance, txg), and applies only records whose (instance, checkpoint_txg) is strictly greater than the root's. The chain continues after the last record covered by the chosen root and stops at the next record whose instance differs from the root's. When the chosen root is the format-time root, it covers no record, and no record is applied.
F5. The replay prefix must be strictly consecutive in sequence number; any record whose checksum fails breaks the chain, and replay stops before it. The stated cost: real mid-ring damage and a torn tail end the same way.
F6. A new instance continues the counter from prefix end + 1 and never resets it.
F7. Administrator rollback is one recovery: the administrator picks an older root R_old; no record after R_old is applied; a new instance id is taken; the rollback and its first new root are published together. If that publish crashes before its root is durable, the next mount must choose the same root, apply the same records, and judge publication the same way as if the rollback had never been started.
F8. The superblock stores a tail value, which recovery must not trust; a stale tail is only an optimization of where to start scanning.
F9. First-round finding under test: when a rollback crashes before its root is durable, the rollback's record written at prefix end + 1 can overwrite the last record covered by the root that a normal recovery would choose; after that, a recovery that derives the chain head only from counters in the ring cannot find where to continue. A second finding: when the last record covered by the chosen root is unreadable on both disks, the same kind of recovery loses records that were durable and whose fsync had returned.
F10. The current implementation sorts all records above the root's watermark by (instance, counter) and starts the chain at the first of them, without checking that it follows the root's last covered record and without checking the instance at the chain head.

Questions:
1. Prefix end during rollback is not defined anywhere. Read it once as the counter covered by R_old and once as the prefix end of the normal recovery of that mount. Under each reading, does the rollback's record overwrite a record that the normal recovery would still need (F9 first finding)?
2. Describe the strongest recovery that does not store any tail or covered counter: it may use F2 (the record after root (i, T) has instance i and checkpoint_txg T + 1), F3 (previous_hash) and the transaction number. On the two F9 findings, does it find the correct chain head?
3. Now let one publish write two records, and let the first of them be torn while the second is durable. Can the strongest recovery of question 2 tell "the missing record was covered by the chosen root" from "the missing record belongs to this replay and was torn"? If not, give two disk states that are byte-for-byte identical where F4 and F5 require different outcomes, and name the one extra quantity that would tell them apart.
4. Does the implementation in F10 apply any record that F4 forbids? Give the shortest sequence.
5. For question 3, which extra quantity would you add, where would it live (root record, record header, superblock), and what single observation would change your choice? Do not decide by bytes saved.
