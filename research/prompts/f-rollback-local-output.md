1. An administrator discovers a data corruption issue that occurred three generations ago. They need to roll back to the third generation to restore correct data. However, under proposal P, the candidate set is limited to only the two most recent generations. The third generation root is excluded even though the blocks it references still exist on disk because the fsync rate is slow enough that no reuse has occurred. The administrator cannot perform the rollback, leading to permanent data loss or inability to recover from the corruption.

2. Against narrowing: an unknown fsync rate means the ring's wall-clock span could be hours or days if the rate is very low. For example, at 1 fsync per second, a 48-slot ring spans 48 seconds. At 0.1 fsync per second, it spans 8 minutes. Narrowing to K=2 would prevent rollback to the third generation even when blocks are still intact, which is possible in slow-rate scenarios. For narrowing: the proposal assumes typical fsync rates are high, making the span short. However, the unknown rate makes this assumption unreliable. The stronger case is against narrowing because the rate is not pinned, so the ring might carry human-timescale data, and narrowing removes valid rollback options without evidence the span is short.
