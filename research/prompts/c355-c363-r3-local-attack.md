THE OPEN QUESTION

Two of the formula's terms, called pending-delete occupancy and committed reservation, are pool-wide scalars: each one is a single number for the whole storage pool, not one number per device. Nowhere in the project is it written whether the byte count recorded for either one represents the logical size of the affected data counted once (as if it had exactly one physical copy), or the sum of the physical bytes occupied by every replica of that data across every device that holds one. Call the first reading the one-copy convention and the second the sum-of-copies convention. Your task is to work out, location by location, what each convention would require changing, and whether either change breaks something else stated in the facts below.

FACT 1. The admission formula reads: available equals, summed over every device, (capacity minus allocated minus unreclaimable minus defer-queue pending-release minus mount-period commitment minus orphaned-root exclusive holding), minus pending-delete occupancy, minus committed reservation, minus the checkpoint reserve pool. This is the authoritative text, in decision D28 (mount-period commitment) settled item 1, line 19 of its source file. Exactly one other place in the whole set of decision files repeats this same formula text: decision D3 (space allocation) settled item 4, line 82 of its source file, whose own surrounding text (line 78) says explicitly "the authoritative text is in D28 settled item 1; cite that one, not this one" and calls its own copy of the formula "only a pointer and a conclusion sentence". A gate check described in FACT 4 requires these two copies of the formula text to match each other character for character, after stripping whitespace and backticks.

FACT 2. The same settled item's own text (decision D28 settled item 1, line 24) explains why some of the formula's terms are summed by device and some are not: the six terms inside the parentheses (including the switch reservation and the orphaned-root exclusive holding that live inside mount-period commitment) are summed by device because they are physical quantities, each replica of a unit lands on one specific device, and one device being full cannot be made up for by space free on a different device. The last three terms, pending-delete occupancy, committed reservation, and the checkpoint reserve pool, are pool-wide commitments instead, and are not summed by device; each appears in the formula as a single scalar. The same sentence adds that this division is what decides, in decision D5 (snapshot / space accounting mechanism) settled item 4, which statistics must carry a device dimension. A separate settled item in the same decision (decision D28 settled item 3, line 73) and a separate decision about striping policy (decision D2 settled item 12, line 194) both state that the first version of the filesystem places exactly two physical replicas of every unit, one on each of two devices, using one location entry per replica.

FACT 5. This project's own tracked-issues list carries an entry, identifier C375, opened 2026-09-17, titled "pending-delete occupancy and committed reservation have no ruling on whether they are recorded per one copy or per the sum of copies". Its description: settled item 4's item 4 and item 6 (FACT 3) are both marked not carrying a device dimension; nowhere in the whole repository is it written whether they record bytes as one copy or as the sum of all copies, verified 2026-09-17 with a search for the term "committed reservation" across the whole knowledge-base directory, excluding the change-history log. In the very first transaction both rows are written as zero, which gives no clue either way. When each unit's two copies land on two devices (FACT 2), and admission is judged by a conjunction evaluated separately per device, these two pool-wide statistics have to be apportioned across each device somehow: recording them as one copy means deducting the full recorded amount from every device; recording them as the sum of copies means deducting half of the recorded amount from every device, for the first version's two-device case. Picking the wrong one either lets a write through on a cell where a tombstone container cannot actually land on some particular device, breaking decision D3 (space allocation) settled item 2's stated promise that "the deletion path still does not request space", or it falsely refuses a cell that should have been allowed. Its stated falsifiability criterion: once the wording settles on a unit convention, or gives both statistics a device dimension instead, which would change the first transaction's accounting row count, an admission cross-check on the specific cell "two devices with 7 slots each, ckpt_cost 4, one tombstone container, requesting 1 unit" must judge that cell a refusal; and, as a self-proof of the check's discriminating power, switching to the other unit convention on that same cell must flip the judgment from refusal to admission. Its listed prerequisites: decision C355's per-device conjunction, and the allocator taking placement per device, tracked separately as issue C368, "the allocator's placement only looks at device 0, and panics when devices are of unequal size". Source: the tracked-issues file, line 330, issue identifier C375.
