1. A scrub operation scans 10000 blocks at 100000 blocks per second taking 0.1 seconds. With K set to 3 (1.077ms window), the publish rate of 2785 per second means each generation lasts 0.36ms. After 1.077ms, the oldest root in the ring is overwritten. The scrub starts at time zero and reads blocks from the initial root. At 0.5 seconds into the scrub, it reads a block that was part of the initial root. Due to K=3, the initial root was overwritten after 1.077ms, and the blocks were reallocated to new data. The scrub reads reused blocks and reports a checksum mismatch or incorrect data as damage.

2. A consumer priced by external event, such as a manual backup trigger, is not addressed by A. A does not specify how to handle external event consumers. If the backup requires a consistent snapshot for 1 hour, but A does not define protection for it and the consumer lacks its own mechanism, the backup would read reallocated blocks. The omission is a hole because external event consumers are not covered by A's categories.

3. A wall-clock consumer like scrub might require a journal replay window longer than the default. If the journal replay window is set to 1 second but scrub takes 10 seconds, blocks pinned by the journal rule are freed after 1 second. The scrub continues reading and encounters reallocated blocks. Even though K is not influenced by wall-clock needs, the journal replay window must be adjusted for wall-clock requirements, but A does not address this indirect influence. The wall-clock need affects other pinning rules, causing failure despite K being unchanged.

4. Consumer c4 has BLANK pricing because it is not implemented. If c4 is later implemented as a wall-clock consumer (e.g., needing 10 seconds of root retention for device recovery), but A assumes it falls under generations pricing, K is set to 2. After 1ms, roots are overwritten. During recovery, the device reappears but the required old roots are gone, causing recovery failure. If c4 was misclassified as generations when it is actually wall-clock, A's rule leads to insufficient protection.

5. A cannot be falsified by any observation. If a wall-clock consumer fails because it lacks its own mechanism, the failure is due to the consumer not following A, not A being wrong. If a consumer has its own mechanism, it works regardless of K. No observation shows A is incorrect because failures are attributable to implementation choices outside A's scope. Per F12, this makes A an article of faith.
